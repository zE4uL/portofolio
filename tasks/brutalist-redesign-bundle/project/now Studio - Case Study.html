§02/ Selected Work/ now Studio/ 2022 → Now

One upload, six stores, zero handoff.

now Studio is the developer platform behind now.gg — a multi-store publishing system for mobile games. From a single build, devs ship to PC, Mac, cloud, LINE, Telegram, and mobile, plug in payments, and watch real-time analytics — all from one dashboard.

I led design end-to-end across three major overhauls — from a scrappy internal tool to a publishing system powering what now.gg calls "the third largest distribution after Apple & Google."

§01 The problem.   What devs were dealing with before now Studio. 2022 / Pre-launch

Mobile game devs ship one build, then spend weeks stitching together five storefronts, three payment SDKs, and a graveyard of analytics dashboards — losing 30%+ of IAP revenue to platform cuts along the way.

Pain · 01
Each store has its own listing format, asset specs, regulations and review cycle. Localization multiplies this.
Pain · 02
Pre-launch testing is manual: zip the build, share over Drive, beg testers, lose feedback in Discord.
Pain · 03
Apple & Google take a 30% cut on IAPs. Indie studios bleed margin on day one.
Pain · 04
Multi-staff studios have no role boundaries — finance sees QA tools, marketing can ship live builds.
01 — REACH

Six storefronts, one upload

Publish across BlueStacks (PC), now.gg (cloud), LINE, Telegram (TON), Mac and mobile from a single build pipeline.

02 — REVENUE

Keep more of every IAP

Drop in nowSDK Payments and bypass the standard 30% storefront cut on supported channels — with localized pricing baked in.

03 — TEST

Ship it before shipping it

Generate test links, gate them by region or tester, and pull qualitative feedback into the same dashboard you publish from.

04 — CONTROL

Roles that actually roll

Granular RBAC across multiple titles. QA never sees billing; marketing never ships a build. Per-app, per-flow.

§02 Three overhauls. Four years.   How the product evolved. 2022 → 2026

Studio didn't arrive fully-formed. It started as an internal admin panel — and grew, version by version, into a publishing system devs trust with their launch day.

v1 · 2022 — INTERNAL
01.

From admin tool, to a platform devs touch.

The legacy version was a Google-Form-shaped data collector; our internal team pasted devs' assets in by hand. v1 turned it into a real self-serve tool — devs could submit, test and publish to now.gg cloud themselves.

  • Self-serve onboarding
  • Cloud (now.gg) publishing
  • Build & asset uploader
  • Basic test-link generation
v2 · 2023 — SDK ERA
02.

SDKs, payments & the analytics layer.

The big one. We launched on BlueStacks app player (PC), wired up the Payments SDK with localized pricing & IAP inventory management, shipped an analytics dashboard, and built a Discord bot to drive traffic to dev pages.

  • BlueStacks PC publishing
  • nowSDK Payments + IAPs
  • Localized pricing matrix
  • Analytics dashboard
  • Discord traffic bot
v3 · 2024+ — MULTI-STORE
03.

Six storefronts. RBAC. One dashboard.

The current era: multi-store publishing across LINE, Telegram (NowStore, TON-powered), Mac, mobile, plus the existing PC + cloud channels. Roles & permissions ship alongside, so multi-staff studios can finally divide work safely.

  • LINE + Telegram + Mac + Mobile
  • Per-store regulations handled
  • Roles & permissions (RBAC)
  • Pricing & IAP templates
  • Real-time session tracking

§03 Three decisions I'd make again. Craft + Tradeoffs
DEC · 01

Strip the publishing flow down to the asks that actually matter.

The first instinct on a dev portal is to ask for everything upfront. We didn't. Each step asks for the minimum needed to advance — and pulls defaults wherever it can: fetch a Play Store listing, auto-fill region pricing, remember the last build's metadata.

The result is a flow that breaks a 40-field form into 4 short, recoverable steps — without losing any of the data the publishing pipeline needs.

"Stop asking devs for things you can fetch. Stop fetching things you don't need yet."
DEC · 02

Treat IAPs & pricing as a spreadsheet, not a form.

Devs don't have 4 IAPs — they have 40, across 30 markets. So I designed pricing as a matrix-first interface: bulk edit by region, copy across tiers, save price & inventory templates for new launches.

The IAP template is the move I'm proudest of — it shaved most of the boilerplate work off every subsequent app a studio published.

DEC · 03

Roles that map to real teams, not abstract policies.

Generic RBAC libraries treat permissions as a flat checklist. Game studios think in roles: QA, Finance, Marketing, Producer. I designed permission scopes around those archetypes — per-app, per-flow — and made the unsafe defaults impossible to choose accidentally.

Net effect: a 12-person studio can onboard a new QA in 2 minutes without giving them keys to the billing console.

§06 What I'd do again, what I wouldn't. Honest self-review

+   What I'd do again

  • Sit in dev calls. The biggest design wins came from watching real studios fumble through builds, not from synthesized personas.
  • Defaults > forms. Pulling listing assets from existing storefronts cut friction more than any flow optimization I tried.
  • Templates as a first-class object. IAP templates, role templates, listing templates — they compounded onto every new app.
  • Design with eng leads in the room. The publishing pipeline has dragons. Co-designing with eng kept the UI honest.

−   What I'd change

  • Ship the analytics view earlier. Devs asked for it from v1. We shipped it in v2. They were right.
  • Roles before scale. RBAC arrived in v3 — it should have been in v2 once multi-staff studios started joining.
  • Build the design system in parallel. I patched components per-flow for too long; consolidating later cost weeks.
  • Document the publishing logic. A flow this branchy needs a living state diagram. We finally have one. We should have started with one.
